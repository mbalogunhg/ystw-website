Dubai, United Arab Emirates

Dubai: desert, souks and the view from the top

Visited October 2015Our rating: 5/5We paid for this trip ourselves

A city that keeps adding attractions. Our tips on what was worth it, and where we should have eaten instead.

Good for: First-timers, families and anyone who likes to keep busy

Where we stayed

Rose Rayhaan by Rotana is a decent four-star; one of us thought it was a three-star pretending. It served its purpose: a short taxi ride from the main sights, food that was fine, a gym and a rooftop pool.

What we did

A boat cruise along the coast is a great way to see the city's landmarks, including the Burj Al Arab and Atlantis.

A desert safari is a must. Quad biking is fun, but dune bashing is the real thrill; hold on tight and don't look straight ahead if you get travel sick. Ours ended with a short camel ride and an evening show at a desert camp. These are usually sold as a package.

Wild Wadi Water Park is a great day out for all ages, especially the Jumeirah Sceirah slide.

We had dinner at the top of the Burj Khalifa. The service and atmosphere were lovely, but neither the view from the restaurant nor the food quite lived up to expectations. Drinks in the bar have the better view.

Visit the Dubai Museum and Old Dubai to understand how the city grew. Anyone who says Dubai has no culture hasn't been. The gold and spice souks are fun for bargaining, and good for saffron.

Where we ate

Nowhere memorable, and that was our fault for not researching restaurants in advance. Do your homework.

Next time

Dubai brunches, indoor snowboarding, drinks at the Burj Al Arab, Atlantis, and more time in Dubai Mall, including the aquarium.

Our verdict

There is far more to Dubai than malls. Plan your restaurants and you’ll have a brilliant trip.

This review reflects our own visit in October 2015. Hotels, prices and opening times change, so we check the latest details before you book.
