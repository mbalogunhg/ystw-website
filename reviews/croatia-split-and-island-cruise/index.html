Split and the islands, Croatia

Split and a small-ship island cruise with a baby

Visited August 2026We paid for this trip ourselves

A family-friendly city, beautiful little island towns, and one honest warning about cruising with very young children.

Good for: Families, and couples who like slow island-hopping

Getting there (the hard way)

Our trip started badly, and none of it was Croatia's fault. A serious accident on the motorway held us up for nearly two hours, our airport meet-and-greet parking firm couldn't find our booking, and the car park barriers failed. We missed our flight. We found an alternative from London Luton, which was delayed by bad weather, and landed close to midnight with a very tired baby. The only food open was a McDonald's.

The lesson: on travel day, leave far more time than you think you need, and keep the parking firm's number handy.

Split

Split airport is small, modern and easy, with a quick immigration queue and friendly staff. A taxi to our hotel took about 30 minutes and cost just under €90. Ride-hailing apps work well too.

We stayed at Hotel Park Split. It's sold as five-star but feels a little tired and dated, and our junior suite was smaller than we expected.

What impressed us most was how well Split looks after families. The big shopping centre has several baby shops with familiar brands, soft play, clean toilets and baby-changing facilities, and staff went out of their way to help. Supermarkets such as Lidl and Spar have plenty of fresh fruit. Prices are close to the UK's: not cheap, not crazy. It feels safe. And the tiramisu bar in the City Centre One shopping centre is excellent.

The island cruise

We spent part of the trip on a small cruise ship with Katarina Line. The boat is a little old, but every cabin has its own bathroom with good water pressure, and the saloon and bar are comfortable. The crew were lovely and our host was brilliant.

We stopped at a different town every day, including Hvar. Even the smallest towns are beautifully kept. At one stop we swam from a small, easy rock-and-sand beach into clear, shallow, refreshingly cold water.

Two things to know. The way the boats dock, often alongside other boats, means getting on and off isn't easy with small children; we'd say it suits children of about nine and older. And the crew expect a large tip at the end, around 150 per guest by our reckoning, which we felt was too much for the service.

Our verdict

Croatia is a great family destination. Do the island cruise when the children are a bit older.

This review reflects our own visit in August 2026. Hotels, prices and opening times change, so we check the latest details before you book.
