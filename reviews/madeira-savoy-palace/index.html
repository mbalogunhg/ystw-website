Madeira, Portugal

Savoy Palace, Madeira: a lovely hotel, with a few things to know first

Visited February 2025 and June 2026We paid for this trip ourselves

Two stays at Savoy Palace in Funchal, both with a baby. Great service, pools and location; food that is good value but rarely exciting.

Good for: Couples and families with young children who want a resort-style city hotel

Getting there

Madeira's airport is named after the island's most famous son, Cristiano Ronaldo, and it has a reputation for strong winds and tricky landings. Both times we flew in, the weather was kind and we landed smoothly.

Travelling with a baby helped at the airport: staff waved us through a faster immigration lane. We booked a private transfer through Holiday Extras both times. The driver was friendly, helped with the bags and the drive to Funchal takes about 25 minutes, with mountains on one side and the ocean on the other. He suggested taking one of the island's cable cars for the views.

The airport itself is small, and the lounge is one small room with no baby-changing facilities.

Where we stayed

Savoy Palace is a big, good-looking hotel with a warm welcome and a lovely lobby. On our first visit we arrived at midday, two hours before check-in, and the room wasn't ready. Fair enough, but nobody could tell us when it would be, and emails we had sent ahead about a cot and baby bath had gone unanswered until we complained. When the room was ready, it had twin beds, which made no sense for two adults and a baby. The hotel moved us to a bigger room within half an hour, but it had no bathtub, so the baby bath we had asked for didn't really work.

On our second visit things went more smoothly. The rooms are a good size, the mini-fridge is small but manageable, and there is a small shop across the road for essentials and a supermarket in the mall, a five-minute drive or 20-minute walk away. The lifts are very slow.

There are three pools: a heated indoor pool, a small but sensibly shallow children's pool outside, and a main pool with good loungers and cabanas. The outdoor pools aren't heated. A cabana is worth it, because the price comes back as a food and drink allowance you can spend at the poolside restaurant.

The kids' room is clean and always staffed, fine for babies who can crawl, though most of the activities suit children over four. The 24-hour gym is a decent size, but the resistance machines are all cable-based rather than proper weights. Room service was inconsistent.

Where we ate

Alameda, the open-air restaurant by the pool, was our favourite on both trips: great service, and the club sandwich, burger and picanha burger are all good. The pizza needed longer in the oven. Galáxia Skyfood, on the top floor, has a beautiful room, a great view and excellent service. Terreiro, the outdoor restaurant, is hard to find (you leave the hotel and go through a gate with your key card) but has a lovely atmosphere and good sea bass.

Pau de Lume, on the ground floor, is fine; skip the rice dishes. Orchidaceae, the Italian-style restaurant, was poor. Breakfast has a good spread, but a lot of food sits out and after five or six days it gets tired. Stick to the egg station and the pancakes.

Outside the hotel, Taj Mahal round the corner is authentic and very good, if a little pricey for Indian food, and World of Asia is a good takeaway. Beef and Wines across the road is one for meat lovers.

What we'd do differently

Book a larger room or a residence with a kitchen, a separate living area and a full bath. Research restaurants outside the hotel before you go. And if you have children at school age, June is a great time to visit because the hotel is quieter.

Our verdict

A very nice hotel for service, location, facilities and looks. The food is good value but not spectacular, so plan some meals out.

This review reflects our own visit in February 2025 and June 2026. Hotels, prices and opening times change, so we check the latest details before you book.
